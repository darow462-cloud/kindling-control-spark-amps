Using KindlingControl

KindlingControl talks to your Positive Grid Spark amp over Bluetooth. It works with the Spark 40, MINI, GO, 2 and NEO. This guide walks through everything in the app, from first connection to saving your own presets.

Getting started

What you need

  • The app: the desktop app (Windows, macOS, Linux) or the Android app, from the releases page. An iPad and iPhone app is planned.
  • Bluetooth switched on, and on macOS, Bluetooth permission for KindlingControl (System Settings → Privacy & Security → Bluetooth).
  • Developers can also run KindlingControl in Chrome or Edge from the source code (npm run serve, see the README). Safari, Firefox and every browser on iPhone and iPad can't do Bluetooth.

Connecting

  1. Close the official Spark app on your phone or tablet, or switch that device's Bluetooth off. The amp only talks to one app at a time.
  2. Switch the amp on.
  3. Press Connect in the top bar, choose your amp from the list (for example "Spark 40 BLE" or "Spark 2"), and confirm.
  4. KindlingControl reads the amp's name, firmware, serial number and battery status, then shows the tone the amp is playing right now.
  5. The Bluetooth symbol next to the logo shows the connection: grey when not connected, flashing amber while connecting, green when connected, red if connecting failed. On wider screens it also shows the amp's name; on a phone, touch and hold (or hover) it to see the text.

Seeing "Paired" next to the amp in the list is normal: it just means your computer already knows the amp, for example from Bluetooth audio.

Press Disconnect when you're done, or simply close the app.

Trying it without an amp

KindlingControl has a built-in simulated amp for developers. Run it from the source code and add ?mock=Spark%202 to the address, for example http://localhost:8787/?mock=Spark%202, then press Connect. Use ?mock on its own for a Spark 40. The desktop app starts with the simulator with npm run desktop:mock. Nothing you do there reaches a real amp.

The Tone screen

This is where you play. From top to bottom: the preset buttons, the signal chain, the amp, then the pedals.

Presets and banks

  • Buttons 1–4 switch between the presets stored on your amp. The lit button is the current one, and the preset's name and description appear next to the buttons.
  • On the Spark 2, which stores eight presets in two banks, a Bank 1 / Bank 2 switch appears above the buttons. Changing bank keeps your position: if you're on preset 3, you go to preset 3 in the other bank.
  • If you change preset or bank on the amp itself, the app follows.
  • Refresh reads the current tone from the amp again, which is handy if something looks out of sync.

Undo, Redo and A/B comparison

The editing bar appears once a tone is loaded. Undo reverses your latest edits; Redo reapplies them. Quick movements of the same knob count as one step. Keyboard shortcuts are Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z); they do not override Undo while typing into a field.

A and B start as two copies of the loaded tone. Edit A, then switch to B and edit it separately; the two variants keep independent histories. Copy A → B (or Copy B → A) replaces the other variant with your current sound. Loading a new preset starts a fresh comparison.

Speed: when two tones use the same amp and pedal models, only changed knobs and switches are sent over Bluetooth. Switching models or altering the chain still requires a slower full-preset transfer.

Safety: fast changes affect only the live sound, while complete restores use the temporary slot. Neither path overwrites stored hardware presets. Use Save… explicitly to keep the sound. Because fast knob commands are not acknowledged, press Refresh if the amp and screen disagree. A/B history is in memory only and does not persist after closing the app.

The signal chain

The strip under the presets shows your guitar's path through the effects, from IN to OUT: noise gate → compressor or wah → drive → amp → modulation or EQ → delay → reverb. A red light means that block is on. Tap a block to jump to its controls.

The amp

  • Choose the amp model from the menu on the left of the amp head. Under the name you'll see which real amp it's based on, where that's known.
  • After a model change, the app checks what the amp is actually playing. If the amp does not acknowledge the quick change, or acknowledges it but keeps the old model, the app tries loading the edited tone into the temporary slot. Nothing in the stored hardware banks is overwritten; use Save… if you want to keep the result.
  • The knobs are laid out like the amp's top panel: Gain, Bass, Middle, Treble, Volume.
  • ON/OFF bypasses the amp model; the red jewel light shows when it's on.
  • Turning a knob on the amp itself moves the matching knob on screen.

The pedals

The other six blocks are shown as pedals, in two rows that follow the signal order:

  • Into the amp: noise gate (1), compressor or wah (2) and drive (3).
  • A badge between the rows marks the amp (4). Tap it to jump back to the amp controls.
  • After the amp: modulation or EQ (5), delay (6) and reverb (7).

On each pedal:

  • The footswitch at the bottom turns it on or off. The red light at the top shows its state, and a switched-off pedal is dimmed.
  • The nameplate is a menu: pick a different model of the same type, say a Tube Drive instead of a Booster. It also shows the real gear the model is based on.
  • Some settings are switches (like a delay's BPM sync) or selectors (like the delay mode or the reverb type) instead of knobs.

Using the knobs

  • Drag up or right to turn a knob up, down or left to turn it down. Hold Shift while dragging for fine adjustments.
  • Double-click (or double-tap) a knob to reset it to the middle (5).
  • Exact values: type a number from 0 to 10 in the field under a knob. A dot or comma is accepted for decimals. Use the mouse wheel over a knob to adjust by 0.1; hold Shift for 0.01 steps. Values outside 0–10 are clamped.
  • With the keyboard, Tab to a knob and use the arrow keys. Page Up and Page Down make bigger steps.
  • Values run from 0 to 10, as in the official app. Changes are sent to the amp as you turn.

Creating and saving presets

New

Press New, give the preset a name, and choose to start from the current sound or a clean, blank preset. The new preset goes straight onto the amp so you can hear it while you shape it, and it's marked not saved. It uses the amp's temporary slot, so nothing stored on your amp changes yet.

Save…

When you like what you hear, press Save…. You can change the name, add a description, and choose where it goes:

  • Save to My presets on this device keeps it in the app's library (see the Presets tab).
  • Store on the amp puts it in one of the amp's preset slots (on a Spark 2, in either bank). This replaces what's stored in that slot, and the app warns you before it does.

You can do either or both. Save always uses what the amp is playing at that moment, including any knobs you turned on the amp itself.

If you try to switch presets while a new preset isn't saved, the app asks first, so you don't lose it by accident.

The Presets tab

Your saved presets, kept on this device. Each one shows its name, optional artist and song, guitar association, amp model and when you saved it.

Use Search to find a preset by name, artist, song, tag or guitar. You can filter by Artist, Guitar or ★ Favorites. Click the star on a preset card to add or remove it from favorites.

Details opens the preset organizer. Enter an artist, song, tags separated by commas and an optional guitar profile. These notes live in your local library; editing them never changes the sound sent to the amp.

Guitar profiles… lets you add, edit or delete instruments with their tuning, pickups, selected Voice and notes. Associate a profile with a preset using Details. Deleting a guitar leaves all presets intact and only clears their association.

Save… also has an optional expandable Artist, Song, Tags and Guitar section, so you can organize a tone as you save it to My presets. These labels are not transmitted to your amplifier.

Back up entire library… saves every local preset, its tags, favorites and saved date, plus all guitar profiles into one JSON file. Use Restore library backup… on another Portable build to merge those records into its local library. You'll see a preview and confirmation before import. Identical items are skipped, and conflicting entries are imported as new copies rather than overwriting your existing sounds. This does not write to the Spark amplifier.

  • Load plays the preset on the amp, using the temporary slot. Your stored presets aren't touched. To make it one of the amp's presets, press Save… and choose a slot.
  • Export downloads the preset as a .json file, including optional artist/song/tags/favorite data and a copy of its assigned guitar profile. Original Spark preset fields remain unchanged, and this extra information is ignored by the amp.
  • Delete removes it from this device only; presets stored on the amp aren't affected.
  • Import preset JSON… adds ordinary preset files from other Spark tools, or restores the optional organizer and guitar details from a KindlingControl export. You can pick several files at once.
  • Export current tone saves whatever the amp is playing right now as a file.

The library lives in this browser or app. It isn't shared between devices, and private or incognito windows forget it when they close (the tab tells you if it can't store data). Export anything you want to keep safe.

Sharing presets online

The presets hub on kindlingcontrol.app is where players swap tones. Browsing and downloading needs no account.

Get a preset

  1. Find one on the hub and press Download. You get a .json file.
  2. In KindlingControl, open the Presets tab and choose Import JSON….
  3. It appears in My presets. Load it to hear it; nothing stored on your amp changes until you choose Save….

Share one of yours

  1. In the Presets tab, press Export next to the preset (or Export current tone).
  2. On the hub, choose Share a preset and sign in with your email address. You'll get a sign-in link by email; there's no password.
  3. Upload the file, add a description if you like, and agree to share it under CC0, which lets anyone use and change it.

Your email address is only used to send sign-in links and is never shown. Other players see the display name you choose. You can delete your shared presets at any time. If a preset looks wrong or offensive, use Report this preset on its page; presets with several reports are hidden until a moderator checks them.

Reordering the chain (experimental)

The Spark appears to use a fixed effect order. KindlingControl lets you try a different order and tells you what your amp does with it.

  1. Press Reorder chain under the signal chain.
  2. Drag the blocks into a new order. Or tap a block and use the ← → keys.
  3. Press Apply, or Cancel to leave everything as it was.

The new order is tried in the amp's temporary slot, so nothing stored changes. The app then reads back what the amp is playing and tells you whether the amp kept your order or put it back to the standard one. If it was kept, listen to check the sound really changed. The amp may store an order it doesn't actually use.

Tuner

On the Tuner tab, switch Tuner mode on and play a single string. The big letter is the nearest note; the needle and the cents read-out show how far off you are, and the needle turns green when you're in tune. Switch it off to go back to playing.

The tuner works on the Spark GO, MINI, 2 and NEO. The Spark 40 doesn't send tuner readings to apps.

Console

For the curious: a live list of every message between the app and the amp, decoded into plain words. Tap a line to see its raw bytes. Tick Hide param / tuner spam to hide the constant stream of knob and tuner messages.

On the web and desktop apps, Pop out opens the Console in its own window, so you can watch the messages while you turn knobs on the Tone screen. Close that window, or press Bring it back on the Console tab, to return it. It closes along with the app.

Copy puts the whole Console on the clipboard as plain text, oldest message first, with the raw bytes of each message. Paste it into a bug report when something goes wrong. It can include preset names, so look it over before you share it.

You can also send a message yourself. That's mainly for people working out the amp's protocol. Messages starting with 02 only ask the amp for information. Messages starting with 01 change settings on the amp, so use them with care.

Separate a song into six instrument tracks (Windows experimental)

The native Windows Portable build can split your own audio file into separate drums, bass, vocals, guitar, piano and other WAV files using HTDemucs 6-stem. This is machine-learning inference on your computer, not an EQ filter or cloud service. The model weights (~84 MB) are downloaded once from Hugging Face and then cached for future uses.

  1. Open Stems and choose a WAV, MP3, FLAC, M4A or other listed input audio file.
  2. Choose the output folder. Each job uses its own new subfolder so it does not overwrite existing WAVs.
  3. Press Separate into 6 tracks. For the first run, allow the model download and initial shader setup. A Vulkan-capable GPU and adequate memory are recommended. The process may take some time; the app shows engine status instead of claiming a precise percentage.
  4. After success, use individual audio players to listen to each stem. Use Open WAV output folder to edit or mix them in a DAW. The individual players are for auditioning and do not offer sample-accurate synchronized mixing.
  5. Press Analyze guitar stem to import the generated guitar WAV into the Analyze tab as the reference. Record your own guitar or load a recording there for comparison.

Privacy and safety: the song and generated WAV files stay on your computer; no audio is uploaded to a cloud source separation service. The first-run model download needs internet. Canceling removes only the unfinished run's new folder, not the selected destination or prior finished files. You choose where to save the WAV files.

Limitations: in dense metal mixes, the isolated guitar may still contain bass or cymbals, and several guitars may be combined into one guitar stem. The six outputs are estimates, not the original studio multitrack session. Browser and mobile builds currently do not include the native Windows engine.

Analyze guitar tone (experimental)

The Analyze tab is an optional, entirely local tool for comparing a reference riff with your guitar track. It does not modify the amp, presets or Bluetooth connection, and cannot determine exact settings from a recording.

  1. Load a reference audio file (WAV, MP3 or other audio supported by Chromium). Prefer an isolated guitar recording rather than a complete song containing drums, bass and a mastered mix.
  2. Load your own guitar recording, or choose a microphone / audio interface and press Record guitar. Recording requires microphone permission, is never automatic, and stops at 30 seconds. The app asks the system for the unprocessed input, but device drivers may still add processing.
  3. Use each position slider to select the same eight-second musical passage in both tracks. The plotted spectrum shows the frequency distribution relative to each track's own strongest frequency; absolute recording volume is shown separately in RMS and peak dBFS.
  4. Compare the six ranges (sub, bass, low mids, mids, presence and air) and listen. The guidance helps you decide whether to check Bass, Middle, Treble, pickup Voice or compression, but is not an automatic preset or an isolated-guitar detector.

Privacy: imported audio, recordings and computed data remain in memory on your device. They are never uploaded or saved to a preset. Closing the app clears the audio. Use headphones to avoid microphone feedback. If the recording device is unavailable, you can record in another application and import its file.

File import has a 40 MB size limit. Your decoder's supported formats depend on the installed Chromium/operating system; WAV and MP3 are the most broadly compatible. No automatic guitar-source separation is performed.

Capture

Decodes an Android Bluetooth log (btsnoop_hci.log) recorded while the official Spark app was connected, so you can see exactly what it sends. Everything is decoded on your device, and nothing is uploaded. That's how features the app doesn't support yet get worked out.

The capture guide explains how to record the log on your phone.

Troubleshooting

Android says "App not installed"
Android refuses the APK when another copy of KindlingControl is still on the phone, even if you uninstalled it from your main apps. Look for it in Private space, a work profile, other users on the phone (or Secure Folder on Samsung), and remove it there too. An archived app (shown with a cloud icon) also still counts: uninstall it fully. If you installed the app from Google Play before, uninstall that copy before installing the APK from the releases page. Also check you downloaded the .apk file: phones can't install the .aab.
My amp doesn't appear in the list
Make sure the amp is on and the official Spark app is closed. Every device that could be connected to the amp needs it closed, or Bluetooth off. Then try again. On macOS, check that your browser has Bluetooth permission.
"This browser has no Web Bluetooth"
Use Chrome or Edge, or install the desktop or Android app. On iPhone and iPad no browser supports Web Bluetooth.
Connection failed
Open the Console tab: the lines starting with bluetooth: show each step, and the error says which one failed. Switch the amp off and on, close other apps that might be connected to it, and press Connect again. On a Mac, if it keeps failing, disconnect the Spark as an audio device in System Settings → Bluetooth (don't choose "Forget"), then try again.
"GATT operation failed for unknown reason"
Usually seen on macOS. The app already retries with a different write mode by itself. If it still fails, power-cycle the amp and reconnect. If you report it, include the Console lines.
The amp model didn't change
The app checks the live preset after a model change. If the amp did not apply the quick change, it tries uploading the edited tone to the temporary slot. If that also fails, check the red error lines in the Console and include your amp model and firmware version when reporting it.
The screen doesn't match the amp
Press Refresh to read the current tone from the amp again.
My saved presets disappeared
The library is stored per browser and per app, and private windows don't keep it. Clearing site data removes it too. Export presets you want to keep.

Settings and About

The gear button in the top bar opens Settings. In released apps it holds the Send anonymous error reports switch (see Privacy and error reports) and the Send Console log… button (see Sending your Console log). The About part shows which version and which app you're using (include both when you report a problem), with links to the source code, the issue tracker and this guide.

Privacy and error reports

KindlingControl has no accounts and keeps your presets on your device. The desktop and mobile apps can send a short, anonymous error report when something goes wrong, so the problem can be found and fixed. They only do this if you agree.

The first time you open the app, it asks Help improve KindlingControl? Choose Send error reports or No thanks. Nothing is sent before you answer, and if you close the question without choosing, it asks again next time. To change your answer later, open Settings (the gear in the top bar) and tick or untick Send anonymous error reports. The setting is kept on this device.

What a report contains: which app it is (desktop, Android, iPhone/iPad), the desktop operating system, the app version, the error message and the place in the code where it happened, and whether this is the first time your app has reported that particular error. Before anything is sent, names in quotes, IDs, serial numbers, addresses and file paths are removed from the message.

What is never sent: any ID for you, your device or your amp, your IP address or location, preset or amp names, or anything you play. To tell whether an error is new for your app, the app remembers which errors it has already reported, on your device only. Development builds and the simulated amp never send reports.

When the same error is reported by more than one app, it is posted as a public issue on the project's GitHub page so it can be fixed. That issue contains only the report contents above.

Sending your whole Console log is a separate, one-off choice with its own preview: see Sending your Console log.

Sending your Console log

If something goes wrong, the Console usually shows why. In released apps you can send it to the project: open Settings (the gear in the top bar) and press Send Console log…. It works whether or not error reports are switched on, because it only happens when you ask for it.

  1. Reproduce the problem first, so the Console has it (the log keeps the newest 300 lines).
  2. Press Send Console log…. A preview shows exactly what will be sent.
  3. Optionally say what happened, and give the number of your GitHub issue if you opened one.
  4. Press Send, or Cancel to send nothing.

What is removed: from the text of each line, names in quotes (preset and device names), the amp's name and serial number, IDs, e-mail addresses and file paths, the same way as for error reports. What is kept: the raw bytes exchanged with the amp, because those are what make the log useful. They can contain your preset names and the amp's serial number.

The log goes to the project's error-report service, and a public GitHub issue is opened that links to it. That issue shows only the app, version and number of lines; the log itself and your note are not public. No ID for you or your device, your IP address or your location is sent. At most 3 logs can be sent per session.

Getting help

Found a bug, or something doesn't work with your amp? Please open an issue on GitHub.

Include your amp model, your device, the app version (in Settings → About), and any red lines from the Console. In released apps you can also send the whole Console log and give it your issue's number.

If KindlingControl is useful to you, you can support it with the yellow Buy me a coffee button in the top bar of the web and desktop apps.
